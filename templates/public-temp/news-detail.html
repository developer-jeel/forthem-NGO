89 Animals Rescued as Kolhapur Flooding Reaches Decade-High Levels

Three rescue boats, 45 volunteers, and 14 partner organisations joined forces as floodwaters submerged over 80 villages in Kolhapur district.

89Animals rescued
1,400Displaced persons reached
6 hrsResponse activation time
45Volunteers deployed

At 4:30 AM on August 14th, 2025, Unke Liye's Rapid Response Coordinator, Rahul Nimkar, received the first distress alert from our network contact in Panchganga village, Kolhapur district. By 7:00 AM, three inflatable rescue boats were already on water.

The 2025 monsoon brought Kolhapur its heaviest rainfall in a decade — 312 mm in 48 hours. The Panchganga and Warna rivers breached their banks simultaneously, inundating 82 villages and displacing approximately 28,000 residents.

Animal Rescue Operations

Among the most urgent priorities were the animals — livestock left behind on rooftops, street dogs trapped on dividers, and household pets stranded as families were evacuated. Our team coordinated with the Maharashtra SPCA and district animal husbandry officials to execute a parallel animal rescue track alongside the human relief operations.

By August 18th — four days into the operation — 89 animals had been rescued:

  • 38 cattle and buffalo — evacuated from 12 submerged farms with coordination from animal husbandry officers
  • 27 dogs and 14 cats — rescued from rooftops, debris piles, and collapsed buildings
  • 10 goats — recovered from stranded farmers in Kolewaadi village
"The water came in within minutes. We couldn't even think about our animals. Unke Liye's team came at dawn and brought our cattle back. They saved our livelihood." — Ramchandra Patil, farmer, Panchganga village

Human Relief Efforts

Simultaneously, our human relief operation supplied 1,400 displaced persons with dry ration packets (rice, dal, oil), drinking water pouches, emergency medicines, and waterproof blankets. Unke Liye operated three distribution camps in coordination with the District Collector's office.

Ongoing Relief and How You Can Help

As of August 19th, relief operations remain active. The immediate rescue phase is complete, but sustained relief (food, medical care, sanitation kits) continues for approximately 620 families who cannot return to their damaged homes.

Unke Liye has a funding gap of ₹4.8 lakhs to complete Phase 2 operations through September. All donations go directly to supplies.
